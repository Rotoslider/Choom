Glass Studio

Clips, looks and sequences for the Chooms on the Looking Glass

Loading the Chooms…

Eight frames from first to last. Flags are hints from the automatic checks: background (the black lifts: fog, a glow, a flash), push-in (she grows: the camera moved), color, seam (the loop doesn't close), cut (a new shot). Keys on a card: K keep, D drop, R re-roll, Space play.

Drag a clip into On the glass to add it, out to take it off, or onto another clip to swap them. Hover to play, double-click to watch it large. Changes reach the glass when you build.

Plan for many looks at once

Tick the actions and the looks to plan them in. Each look gets the ticked actions it doesn't have yet (a clip she already has is never planned again) and that come in its pose. Preview shows what each look gets and how long it all renders; nothing is planned until you click Plan.

Actions

Looks

Write your own clip

Say what she does, start to finish, in one or two sentences. She must end where she began (the Studio adds the camera line and the ending). Five seconds fail least; a dance or a slow turn may want ten or fifteen.

Change of clothes

A clip from one of her looks to another: it starts on the first look's picture and ends on the second's, and the glass plays it instead of the camera cut when she changes from one to the other. Make one each way (into her night look, and back out in the morning). Say what happens in between, in one or two sentences; one motion in five to seven seconds works best. A change by magic (her motes swirl and the dress becomes the nightgown) renders more reliably than clothes moved by hand, which often needs a few re-rolls.

Planned

Each look is a clean picture of her and the line that describes her in it. Every clip in the look starts and ends on that picture; the subject starts every prompt (say “a plain pure black background”), the keep line follows it in every clip (Genesis: her motes stay on her), and loops finish with the ending.

New look: an outfit, full body, asleep, a place, the empty glass, or a new picture for a look

Klein makes a few versions from one of her pictures; pick one and it becomes a look you can plan clips for. An outfit needs Essentials first, then a dozen or more quiet moments; when she wears it comes from its name, like the glass's other outfits. Full body is for the full-body moves and twirls: start it from her usual look, or from an outfit for that outfit's own full body (the glass cuts to it only while she wears that outfit). Asleep is for the sleep clips; from a night outfit, she sleeps in it. A place is somewhere she goes and stays a while when she asks (sitting by a campfire, in a chair): made from her full body, named by you, with its own loop, quiet moments, and clips to sit down and get up (plan "A place" in it). The empty glass is a black picture for walking on and off (plan "Walking on and off" in her full body); no Klein needed. A new picture for a look she has replaces that look's picture (a truer colour, a better likeness); every clip in the look is planned again from it, and the old takes are kept. Klein can start from any of her looks, or from a picture on your computer.

Say which picture holds what: picture 1 is her, the ones you added are picture 2, 3 and so on in the order chosen (“she now wears the dress from picture 2 and the sandals from picture 3”).

Work

A new Choom on the glass

Start from a picture of her: a render, a portrait, her Choom avatar. Klein cleans it up into a picture of her on black (pick the best of a few), then the Studio makes her still relief so the glass knows her, and her clips come from the library like everyone else's. Her name should match her name in the Choom app, so the glass knows when she talks.

Pause the work list?

Waiting jobs stay waiting, and nothing new starts on the GPU until you resume, even when a scheduled time comes. Checks of clips already made carry on.

Clean up

Measuring…

Never deleted: anything a kept, planned, queued or rendered clip uses, a look's picture, versions a look is waiting on, or a queue a job still uses. Deleting can't be undone.
